Tomato Harvester Robot

A custom 4-DOF robot arm that uses stereo vision and analytic inverse kinematics to locate cherry tomatoes and pick them with a vacuum end effector.

Destination and journey

System Overview

A working prototype that combines a compact 4-DOF arm, a stereo-vision perception pipeline, closed-form inverse kinematics, and a vacuum (shop-vac) end effector.

What It Does

  • Detects cherry tomatoes with a YOLOv11 model, classifies ripeness, and tracks each fruit with a persistent ID across frames.
  • Estimates every tomato's 3D position from stereo disparity (SGBM) and a pinhole camera model, then transforms it into the arm's base frame.
  • Plans a staged straight-line approach (align → pregrasp → contact → retreat → home) and solves it with an analytic 4-DOF inverse-kinematics solver.
  • Drives the vacuum end effector onto the fruit under a manual select-and-approve workflow, guarded by several independent motor safety limits.
  • Records and replays hand-demonstrated trajectories as groundwork for planned imitation learning.

Future Work

  • An elevator for the arm and cameras: Real tomatoes sit higher than the arm's vertical workspace, which forced picking at a shorter depth than the stereo pipeline was tuned for.
  • Full autonomy: Picking currently runs under a manual select-and-approve workflow.
  • Imitation learning: Integrate an ACT policy with inverse kinematics.
